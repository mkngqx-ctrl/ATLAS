ATLAS advantage: S3 paired patient bootstrap

Scientific figures updated for optimized DDH and DRSA, 16 November 2026. Positive MAE reductions favor ATLAS. All 95% intervals are pointwise, conditional on saved fitted models; no multiple-comparison adjustment. Baseline exceptions are shown.

figure 1 headline bootstrap

Headline ATLAS configurations versus the strongest fitted direct comparator at each landmark and target. Relative MAE reduction is 100 times (comparator MAE minus ATLAS MAE) divided by comparator MAE; positive favors ATLAS. Horizontal intervals are pointwise 95% percentile bootstrap intervals from 5,000 paired resamples of the original 300 test patient IDs, with original landmark eligibility applied. Per-patient errors are averaged across saved sampling seeds and training runs before resampling. The direct comparator is fixed by the unresampled updated mean errors, not reselected during resampling. ATLAS uses common Sobol sampling; Transformer is shown for the duration-dependent experiment and GRU for competing risks. The baseline duration-dependent interval includes zero. These intervals condition on saved fits and realized sampling outputs, with no multiplicity or model-selection adjustment; reductions concern prediction error, not mortality risk.

figure 2 duration comparator matrix

Duration-dependent survival comparison for all three ATLAS architectures and all eight fitted direct comparators at four landmarks. Each cell is the percentage reduction in mean MAE relative to the row comparator. The colors use a common -100% to +100% scale centered at zero; blue denotes lower ATLAS error. Hatched cells indicate that the corresponding pointwise 95% paired patient-bootstrap interval for the MAE difference includes zero; lack of hatching does not constitute a simultaneous significance statement. The baseline Transformer/DynamicDeepHit-Lite cell is the only such exception. ATLAS means use common Sobol sampling and nine saved fits. Landmark samples and prediction windows differ, so changes across landmarks do not isolate the effect of additional history. Numerical values are rounded to the nearest whole percentage; full precision and intervals remain in paired_comparisons.csv.

figure 3 competing risk comparator matrix

Competing-risk comparison for both ATLAS architectures and all eight fitted direct comparators, separately for survival, Death_A CIF and Death_B CIF at landmarks 0, 10 and 20. Cells show relative reductions in mean MAE; the common scale and hatching have the same meanings as in Figure 2. All point estimates favor ATLAS, but the baseline Transformer versus Weibull-MLP Death_B CIF interval includes zero. ATLAS uses the common Sobol sampler and five saved training fits; predictions and errors are paired by patient. These are conditional pointwise uncertainty summaries, without adjustment for the displayed family of comparisons. Percentage error reductions are not reductions in clinical event risk.
