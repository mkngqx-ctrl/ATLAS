DURATION- AND CALENDAR-TIME-AWARE SEMI-MARKOV COMPARATOR
2026-10-28

STATUS
Exploratory analysis completed; original archives and manuscript unchanged.

FORMULATION
Separate multinomial logistic models by current live state s:
q_j(s,u,t,x) = softmax_j(beta_sj' phi(u,t,z)), with z=(x-mu_train)/sd_train.
Source time t is the time of S_t; predicted destination is S_(t+1).
Initial state has a separate fitted multinomial regression using covariates only.
Responses include three live states, death and censoring. Destination support is
learned from training observations, not supplied from the generator.

Candidate 1 features: intercept; standardized covariate powers 1 through 4;
run-length indicators for u=2,...,30; source-time indicators for t=2,...,29.
Candidate 2 adds z*v,z^2*v,z*v^2,z^2*v^2 and time-indicator*v interactions,
where v=log(1+u)/log(31). Each source state has its own coefficients.
Penalty: one half times sum of squared coefficients (ridge=1 on summed NLL).
No true coefficients, change points, or oracle probabilities were supplied.
The model is time-inhomogeneous and semi-Markov in the discrete-time augmented
state sense; self-transitions increment u and state changes reset u to 1.

TRAINING AND SELECTION
Original hash-matched Hard24 cohort, 40,000 training and 5,000 validation
patients; original 5,000 held-out test patients. Training has 632,886 transitions
plus 40,000 first states. Absorbing-state repetitions excluded.
Both candidates fitted on training only. Interaction model selected using
validation observed transition log loss: 0.858827 versus additive 0.859853.
No oracle curve scores or test data used in specification selection.
One initial-state fit and six candidate state-specific fits; four selected fits.
All final fits converged with average objective gradient below 1e-7.
An initial unscaled optimizer attempt reached its iteration limit; column
reparameterization improved conditioning without changing model or penalty.
No statistical specification was altered in response to test scores.

PREDICTION
Exact forward propagation over (s,u), separately for every covariate and source
time. At each step, death mass is divided by total remaining observed risk mass
before same-step censor removal, then KM survival is updated. Live mass alone
continues; this matches the archived event-first estimand. No Monte Carlo error.

Lower mean error than all 31 fitted comparison configurations at every landmark:
21 ATLAS, eight original direct, prior current-state Markov, and Cox supermodel.
Oracle sampler is a separate known-generator control, not a fitted comparator.
At baseline, the fitted model is more accurate than the finite-IID oracle sampler;
this does not beat the exact reference, whose error is zero.

UNCERTAINTY
5,000 patient-paired bootstrap resamples, seed 20261028. Differences compare
semi-Markov patient errors with comparator errors averaged over sampling seeds
within training run and then over training runs. Archived patient-error means
recomputed and matched published summaries within 1e-12.
Pointwise 95% percentile intervals, no multiplicity adjustment. Conditional on
fixed fitted models, selected specification, cohort and eligible test population;
not uncertainty over training samples or independent generated datasets.
Intervals favor semi-Markov over all 21 ATLAS configurations at L=0,20,24;
at L=23 only seven (the RNN configurations) have intervals wholly below zero.
Mean differences favor semi-Markov for all 21 at L=23, but GRU and Transformer
comparisons at this landmark remain uncertain by these intervals.

VALIDATION CHECKS
Sparse scaled L-BFGS probabilities agree with independent dense Newton fitting
on a toy problem within 1e-6. Explicit source-time/run-length/terminal indexing
test passed. Augmented propagation matched independent archived reference
recursion at all four landmarks within 1e-13 (known probabilities used only for
unit testing). Conservation, probability range, survival monotonicity, cohort
hash, split disjointness, patient IDs and recomputed MAEs checked.

INTERPRETATION
Duration and calendar time substantially improve this classical comparator.
The current manuscript cannot claim uniform superiority over fitted classical
comparators if this model is included. This is an exploratory specification
comparison on a previously studied benchmark and needs scientific review and
independent implementation verification before submission.
